---
name: fill-gaps-and-restate
description: Completes a rough, half-formed thought the user can't yet put into words and gives it back finished, clearer and recognisably theirs, carried one step on. Use when the user shares an idea, feeling, hunch, brainstorm or creative concept of their own and wants it put into words ("fill the gaps", "help me articulate this", "say what I mean", "what am I trying to say"), or pastes a messy note or voice dictation of their own, even if they don't name the skill. Not for editing, translating or summarising someone else's text, or for answering factual questions.
---

The user has a thought they can't say yet. It has a definite shape, and they'll recognise the right words when they see them. Give them the **finished thought**, what they were reaching for, completed, so they read it and think "yes, that's it, I didn't know I knew it."

Work out first what they're circling. Read everything that's theirs (their key words, images, repetitions, hedges, what they said earlier, and the context they left out because it felt obvious), distrust the obvious reading, and find the one in which the scattered pieces fit.

Then write it back to them, as plain prose. Lead with the sentence they were reaching for, built from their own key words. Follow it with what they didn't manage to say and where the thought goes from where they stopped. Where they wrote "maybe", "I guess" or "not sure", that is a gap to fill: follow it through and settle on the version that holds up.

Format it like so:

```
<the sentence they were reaching for>

<what they didn't manage to say and where it goes, in one or two short paragraphs>
```

Done when the user reads it and recognises exactly what they meant, said better than they could.
